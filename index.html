The Beatitudes

The blessings Jesus spoke at the beginning of the Sermon on the Mount

Matthew 5:3–12

He sat down, and his disciples came to him. He opened his mouth and taught them, saying—

Matthew 5:3
“Blessed are the poor in spirit,for theirs is the Kingdom of Heaven.

The kingdom is not earned by those who feel sufficient. It is received with empty hands—by those who know their need and come to God as they are.

Matthew 5:4
“Blessed are those who mourn,for they shall be comforted.

Jesus does not turn away from sorrow. He draws near to it. No grief offered to him is unseen, and no tear is beyond the reach of his comfort.

Matthew 5:5
“Blessed are the gentle,for they shall inherit the earth.

Gentleness is strength yielded to God. Those who do not grasp for power are promised an inheritance no striving could secure.

Matthew 5:6
“Blessed are those who hunger and thirst after righteousness,for they shall be filled.

The deep longing for what is right is itself met with a promise. God will not leave that hunger empty; he will satisfy it fully.

Matthew 5:7
“Blessed are the merciful,for they shall obtain mercy.

Mercy remembers how much it has received. It looks upon another’s weakness with compassion and becomes a living echo of the mercy of God.

Matthew 5:8
“Blessed are the pure in heart,for they shall see God.

A heart made clean and undivided is turned toward God. Its hope is not merely to know about him, but to behold him.

Matthew 5:9
“Blessed are the peacemakers,for they shall be called children of God.

Peacemakers step into division carrying the family likeness of their Father. They do not simply avoid conflict; they labor for wholeness.

Matthew 5:10
“Blessed are those who have been persecuted for righteousness’ sake,for theirs is the Kingdom of Heaven.

Faithfulness may be costly, but suffering cannot exile anyone from Christ’s kingdom. The kingdom belongs to them even now.

Matthew 5:11–12
“Blessed are you when people reproach you, persecute you, and say all kinds of evil against you falsely, for my sake.Rejoice, and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven. For that is how they persecuted the prophets who were before you.”

When faithfulness to Jesus brings rejection, he calls the rejected blessed. Their suffering is seen, their company is ancient, and their reward with him is great.

These blessings are not spoken from a distance. The King himself came near and gave them to the weary, the hungry, the merciful, and the overlooked.

Scripture: Matthew 5:3–12, World English Bible
